Smart and TNT are two brands of the same company, Smart Communications, which is the wireless arm of PLDT. Sun used to be a third brand, but it was folded into Smart: Sun Prepaid in 2020 and Sun Postpaid in 2022. Today you are really choosing between Smart and TNT.

## How the brands relate

PLDT's own company information says its wireless services are sold under the Smart and TNT brands, while its fixed-line services use the PLDT Home and PLDT Enterprise names. TNT is short for Talk 'N Text, and it is generally positioned as the lower-cost prepaid brand. Smart is the flagship, with prepaid, postpaid and wireless broadband.

| TNT | Current | Smart Communications' budget-oriented prepaid brand |
| Sun | Retired | A former Digitel brand, now part of Smart |

## What happened to Sun

Sun Cellular started as Digitel's mobile brand, and PLDT acquired Digitel in 2011. The brand was then phased out in two steps.

- **October 2020.** PLDT said Smart had completed the rebranding of Sun Prepaid to Smart Prepaid, so Sun Prepaid subscribers became Smart Prepaid subscribers.
- **April 25, 2022.** Sun Postpaid became Smart Postpaid. Smart said existing subscribers could keep their SIM, number and plan inclusions, and it pointed new customers to its Smart Signature plans instead.

> [!NOTE] Still have a Sun SIM?
> It is now a Smart SIM. For plan or billing questions, use Smart's official channels, not old Sun pages or accounts.

## How to choose between Smart and TNT

Because the two share a parent company, the useful differences are practical ones. Use this checklist:

1. **Signal where you live and work.** Coverage varies by area and even by time of day, so test with a SIM before you commit.
2. **The offers you would actually use.** Compare the current data, call and app offers in each brand's official app or website, since promos change often.
3. **Prepaid or postpaid.** Smart covers both, so check what each option includes and requires.
4. **Loading and support.** See which app and channels each brand uses to reload, check your balance and get help.

> [!TIP] Try before you commit
> Opinions about "the better network" often come from one person's neighborhood. A starter SIM from each brand is a cheap way to test the signal at home.
