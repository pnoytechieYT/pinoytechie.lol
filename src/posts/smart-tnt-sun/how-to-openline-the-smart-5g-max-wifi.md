## Understanding Network Locks on Pocket Wi-Fi

Many portable routers sold directly by mobile providers in the Philippines are locked to their own network out of the box. This means the device will only recognize a SIM card from that specific provider.

- **Network restriction:** A locked device will typically show an error or fail to connect if you insert a SIM card from a different provider.
- **Official guidelines:** Providers generally design these gadgets to work exclusively with their own data offers and network infrastructure.
- **Software updates:** Unofficial unlocking methods found online can sometimes interfere with device updates or security patches.

## What to Check Before You Make Changes

Before you search for tools or guides to unlock your pocket Wi-Fi, run through a few basic checks to ensure you actually need to switch networks.

1. **Test the signal where you use it most.** Coverage can vary significantly depending on your exact location, time of day, and whether you are indoors or outdoors.
2. **Review available data promos.** Check the provider's official app or website to see current data offers that might fit your budget better than switching networks.
3. **Check your warranty status.** Modifying firmware or attempting unauthorized unlock procedures can void your device warranty.

> [!NOTE] Check official channels
> For exact details about your device model, warranty terms, and official troubleshooting steps, always refer to the provider's official website or customer support channels.

## Remember the SIM Registration Act

If you do manage to use a different network's SIM card in your device, keep in mind that the SIM Registration Act (Republic Act No. 11934) requires every SIM to be registered with the respective provider before it can activate. SIMs are sold deactivated, and registration is free for all users.

Whether you are using Smart, TNT, or another provider like Globe, TM, or DITO, you must complete the registration process using the official portal of that specific network.
