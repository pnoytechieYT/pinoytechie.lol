MariBank's public materials focus on savings and transfers, but the bank also offers a **loan product available to select users**. The details are not prominently advertised, and eligibility is not automatic. Here is what the available information shows.

## What the Product Is

MariBank's Philippine loan product is a cash loan offered through the app to qualified users. It is distinct from the MariBank Singapore business loan products, which are marketed to SME customers in Singapore and have their own promotional terms.

For Philippine users, the loan appears as an option in the app for accounts that meet MariBank's internal criteria. If the option is not visible, the account has not been selected for the offer.

## Who Qualifies

MariBank does not publish a checklist of eligibility requirements for the Philippine loan. Based on how digital banks typically structure these products, the criteria likely include:

- **Account tenure**: how long the account has been open and active.
- **Transaction history**: deposit consistency, balance levels, and usage patterns.
- **Verification level**: a more fully verified account is more likely to be eligible.
- **Repayment capacity signals**: salary credits or regular incoming transfers may factor into the assessment.

The bank makes the final determination. There is no application process visible to users who have not been pre-selected; the offer appears in the app or it does not.

## What to Check Before Accepting

**Interest rate and effective cost.** The advertised rate is not always the effective annual rate. Check the disclosure for the total amount repayable, including any processing fees.

**Repayment schedule.** The loan term and installment amounts should be clear before you confirm. A shorter term means higher monthly payments but less total interest.

**Late payment terms.** What happens if a payment is missed? Digital banks typically charge a penalty fee and may report to credit bureaus. The terms are in the loan agreement, which is shown before acceptance.

**Impact on your savings.** If you have a MariBank savings account, the loan is a separate product. Defaulting on the loan does not automatically deduct from your savings, but it can affect your credit standing and future access to MariBank products.

## The Alternative

For users who need short-term liquidity and have a MariBank time deposit, breaking the time deposit may be simpler than taking a loan, depending on the early withdrawal terms. MariBank credits time deposit interest upfront, so the cost of breaking a deposit is the interest already paid that may need to be returned or adjusted.

For users with a traditional bank account, a salary loan or credit card cash advance may be cheaper than a digital bank cash loan, depending on the rate. Compare the effective cost, not just the monthly payment.

## What This Post Does Not Cover

MariBank's loan terms can change, and the product is not available to all users. If you see a loan offer in the app, read the full terms before accepting. If you do not see one, there is no public application link to request access—the offer is algorithmic and internal to MariBank.
