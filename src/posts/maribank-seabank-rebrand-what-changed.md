SeaBank Philippines completed its rebrand to **MariBank Philippines Inc.** effective July 31, 2025, following approval from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and the Securities and Exchange Commission [citation:4]. For existing users, the transition raised a practical question: what actually changed?

## The Name and the App

The bank's legal name changed, and the app store listing still shows **"MariBank PH (SeaBank)"** in its title, which is why searches for either name surface the same app [citation:9]. The app itself has been updated to the MariBank interface, but the package name and some backend references retain the SeaBank identifier.

MariBank Philippines president Siew Ghee Kung Lim stated at the time of the rebrand that **only the bank's name has changed** and that all existing products, accounts, and services remain accessible [citation:4].

## What Stayed the Same

**Account numbers** did not change. Existing users did not need to take any action, and deposits remained PDIC-insured up to ₱1,000,000 per depositor [citation:11][citation:15].

**The interest rate structure** also carried over. The 3.25% rate on balances up to ₱1M and 3.75% above that was in place under the SeaBank brand and continued under MariBank [citation:1].

**The free transfer policy** via InstaPay and PESONet remained, though the specifics of the free transfer allowance have shifted over time (see below).

## What Changed or Evolved

**The digital banking license.** In July 2026, MariBank secured a digital banking license from the BSP, transitioning from its previous classification as a rural bank. This made it the country's **seventh digital bank** [citation:11]. The reclassification does not change deposit insurance or account terms, but it places MariBank under the digital bank regulatory framework rather than the rural bank framework.

**Free transfer terms.** The unlimited free transfer model has moved back and forth. At one point the bank experimented with a **50 free transfers per month** cap starting June 1, 2026, before dropping transfer fees entirely again following BSP Circular No. 1238 [citation:12]. The current state is unlimited free InstaPay and PESONet transfers, but the history suggests the policy can change.

**The physical card.** The MariCard, which costs ₱49 for the physical version, is the same product under the new brand. The fee structure for ATM withdrawals and foreign transactions carried over unchanged [citation:6].

## The Corporate Context

MariBank Philippines is part of **Sea Group**, the parent company of Shopee and Garena. The rebrand aligned the Philippine entity with MariBank Singapore, which is one of Singapore's licensed digital banks, both operating under SeaMoney, Sea's fintech arm [citation:4]. For users, this corporate structure explains the ShopeePay integration and the Shopee ecosystem perks, which were present under SeaBank and continue under MariBank.

## What to Tell Someone Who Asks

If a friend or family member used SeaBank and is confused by the MariBank name: their account is the same account, their money is in the same place, and their rates and card work the same way. The only thing that changed is what to call it.
