MariBank's help pages and fee schedules repeatedly mention that limits "vary by account verification level," but the app does not always make the tiers obvious during onboarding. Understanding the difference matters if you plan to move significant amounts or use the card abroad.

## What Verification Actually Controls

Verification level determines three things: your **daily outgoing transfer limit**, your **ATM withdrawal cap**, and access to certain features. MariBank does not publish a single universal limit for all users; the default you receive can differ based on account history and how completely you completed identity verification.

The practical implication: two users with the same balance can have different daily transfer ceilings depending on when they opened their accounts and what documents they submitted.

## The Verification Steps That Matter

The initial sign-up requires a government-issued ID and a selfie for liveness verification. That gets you an account, but it does not necessarily get you the highest limits.

Users who want higher limits typically need to complete additional verification, which may include submitting a second ID or proof of address. The exact requirements are shown in the app under your profile or account settings, and they can change.

## How to Check Your Current Limits

Open the MariBank app and go to **Card Settings**, then **Limits**. This screen shows your ATM withdrawal limit and your POS/online spending limit as separate figures. Your transfer limits are shown in the transfer section of the app, usually before you confirm a transaction.

MariBank's public fee page confirms that ATM withdrawals have a daily cap of **₱100,000 across up to 15 withdrawals**, but the page notes that minimum and maximum amounts "may vary" depending on the ATM and your account. Your actual limit may be lower than the published maximum.

## Raising Your Limits

You can request a limit increase in the app under Card Settings. Most changes apply immediately, though a large jump may prompt additional verification. If you are planning a one-time large transfer, such as a tuition payment or a property deposit, check your limit the day before rather than at the moment you need to send the money.

## What Does Not Change With Verification

The interest rate on your savings does not depend on verification level. A basic verified account earns the same **3.25%** on balances up to ₱1M as a fully verified one. PDIC coverage also applies regardless of tier, up to ₱1,000,000 per depositor.

If your only goal is to park savings and earn interest, basic verification is sufficient. If you plan to use MariBank as a primary transaction account, completing the higher tier is worth the extra steps.
