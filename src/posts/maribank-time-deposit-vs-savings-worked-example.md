MariBank's time deposit pays **3.75% per annum**, while the savings account pays **3.25%** on balances up to ₱1,000,000. The 0.50 percentage point difference looks small, but the way interest is paid makes the comparison less obvious than it appears.

## The Savings Account Option

If you keep **₱50,000** in MariBank Savings for one year at 3.25%, the gross interest is **₱1,625**. After the **20% withholding tax**, you keep approximately **₱1,300**.

The key advantage: the money is fully liquid. You can withdraw any portion at any time without penalty, and interest is credited daily based on your previous day's balance. If you need the funds in month three, you simply withdraw them and keep the interest earned up to that point.

## The Time Deposit Option

Place **₱50,000** in a 1-month or 3-month time deposit at 3.75%. The annualized gross interest would be **₱1,875** if held for a full year, but time deposits are short-term. For a **3-month term**, the gross interest is approximately **₱468.75**, and after 20% tax you keep about **₱375**.

The critical difference: MariBank credits time deposit interest **immediately upon successful placement**, not at maturity. That means the ₱375 appears in your account on day one, while the principal remains locked for the term.

## The Liquidity Cost

If you need the ₱50,000 before the term ends, the time deposit cannot be withdrawn without breaking the term. MariBank's terms on early withdrawal are shown in the app at placement; typically, breaking a time deposit forfeits some or all of the interest, and the upfront crediting complicates the calculation.

For money you are certain you will not touch for one to three months, the time deposit's slightly higher rate and upfront payment are a reasonable trade. For an emergency fund or any balance you might need on short notice, the savings account's liquidity is worth more than the 0.50% difference.

## The Auto-Renewal Trap

MariBank offers auto-rollover, which renews the deposit at maturity for the same term. This is convenient if you want to keep the money parked, but it also means the funds stay locked unless you actively change the setting. If there is any chance you will need the money, leave auto-renewal off or set a calendar reminder before maturity.

## A Practical Split

Many users keep the bulk of savings in the liquid account and place only the portion they are confident about into a time deposit. For ₱50,000, a common approach is ₱30,000 in savings and ₱20,000 in a 3-month time deposit, capturing some of the higher rate without locking the entire balance.
