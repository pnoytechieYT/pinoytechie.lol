MariBank, CIMB, and Tonik are the three names that come up most often when Filipinos compare digital savings options. Each advertises a rate that looks competitive, but the structures differ enough that the headline number rarely tells the full story.

## The Rates Side by Side

MariBank pays **3.25% per annum** on balances up to ₱1,000,000, with **3.75%** on the portion above that. Interest is credited daily, and there is no maintaining balance.

CIMB's UpSave account carries a base rate of **2.30%** effective August 1, 2026, while its FastPlus account pays **0.75%**. CIMB operates under a commercial bank license rather than a digital bank license, despite its app-first model.

Tonik's Solo Stash advertises **4.00% per annum**, the highest base rate among the three, with no conditions attached to the rate itself.

## What the Headline Rates Hide

Tonik's 4.00% is genuinely higher than MariBank's 3.25% for balances under ₱1M. On ₱100,000 held for a year, the after-tax difference is meaningful: Tonik nets approximately **₱3,247** while MariBank nets approximately **₱2,631**. For a saver choosing purely on yield, Tonik wins at that balance.

But rate is not the only variable. MariBank credits interest **daily**, which means compounding happens continuously rather than monthly. CIMB's GSave product pays interest monthly. The frequency difference is small in peso terms but matters for users who watch their balance grow.

## Time Deposits

MariBank offers a **1-month or 3-month time deposit at 3.75%** with a minimum of ₱100, and interest is credited **immediately upon placement** rather than at maturity. That upfront crediting is unusual and effectively gives you the interest before the term ends.

Tonik's time deposit rates vary by term, with a 6-month option advertised at **4.00%** as of August 2026. The catch: the extra return may require maintaining a minimum average daily balance in your Tonik savings account throughout the term.

CIMB's time deposit offerings were not listed at a competitive rate on its public rate page as of October 2026.

## PDIC Coverage

All three banks are PDIC-insured up to **₱1,000,000 per depositor**. There is no safety advantage to choosing one over another on insurance grounds.

## The Practical Verdict

If you are optimizing purely for rate on balances under ₱1M, **Tonik's 4.00%** is the highest base rate among the three. If you value daily interest crediting and a simple structure with no conditions, **MariBank's 3.25%** is straightforward. CIMB sits behind both on rate but has the longest operating history among the three in the Philippine market.

A common approach is to keep the bulk of savings in the highest-rate account and maintain a smaller balance in MariBank for its free transfers and ShopeePay integration. Rates change, so confirm the current figure in each app before moving money.
