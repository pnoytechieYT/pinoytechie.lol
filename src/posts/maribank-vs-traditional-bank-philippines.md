MariBank is often compared to GCash, but the more instructive comparison for savers is against a traditional brick-and-mortar bank.

## Where MariBank Wins

**Interest rate**: MariBank pays 3.25% on balances up to ₱1M, with 3.75% above that [citation:1]. Traditional bank savings accounts in the Philippines typically pay well under 1%. Some premium products require large maintaining balances to earn more [citation:6].

**No maintaining balance**: You can keep ₱0 in MariBank without a penalty [citation:2]. Traditional banks often charge a fee if your balance falls below a required minimum.

**Free transfers**: Unlimited InstaPay and PESONet transfers [citation:2][citation:3]. Many traditional banks charge per transfer or limit free transfers.

**Daily interest crediting**: Interest is credited every midnight [citation:1], so you see the earnings accumulate in real time.

## Where Traditional Banks Still Win

**Physical branches**: MariBank has one branch—the Pagsanjan branch in Laguna, inherited from its rural bank origins [citation:10]. If you need in-person services like notarized documents, manager's checks, or complex account issues, a branch network matters.

**Full product suite**: Traditional banks offer credit cards, auto loans, mortgages, corporate accounts, and trust services. MariBank's product set is narrower: savings, time deposit, debit card, and a loan product available to select users [citation:2].

**Trust inertia**: Forbes ranked MariBank #1 in customer satisfaction, but traditional giants still hold the majority of deposits in the Philippines [citation:5]. Old habits and perceived safety keep money in familiar names.

## The Practical Move

Many users keep a traditional bank account for branch-dependent needs and park savings in MariBank for the rate. It does not have to be either-or.
