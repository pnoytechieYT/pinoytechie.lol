Maya has two parts. The **Maya Wallet** is an e-wallet for payments and pays no interest. **Maya Savings** is a deposit account at **Maya Bank**, a BSP-licensed digital bank, and that is where your money earns interest.

## The interest rate in plain terms

According to Maya Bank:

- The **base rate is 3% a year**, credited daily, on your whole balance.
- You can **boost the rate up to 15% a year** by using Maya for everyday payments and by growing your balance.
- The **bonus interest only applies to the part of your balance up to ₱100,000.**
- Interest is computed on your end-of-day balance, credited the next day, **less 20% withholding tax**.

> [!WARNING] The 15% is not guaranteed
> 15% is the ceiling, not the starting point. The boost depends on activity and campaigns, and Maya can change rates after notifying the public. Use the 3% base for any conservative estimate, and check the interest calculator inside the app for your real rate.

### A worked example

Say you keep ₱10,000 for a full year at only the 3% base rate:

- Gross interest: ₱10,000 x 3% = ₱300
- Less 20% tax: ₱60
- You keep about **₱240**

At a higher boosted rate, the same ₱10,000 would earn proportionally more, but only while the boost applies and only on up to ₱100,000.

## Is the money safe

Maya Bank is regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, and deposits are **insured by the PDIC up to ₱1,000,000 per depositor**. That protection covers the savings account at the bank. Money sitting in the Maya **Wallet** is e-money and is not a bank deposit. To learn how PDIC coverage works across banks, read our [PDIC and BSP safety guide](/blog/maribank-pdic-bsp-safety/).

## How to start Maya Savings

1. [Upgrade your Maya account](/blog/how-to-open-and-upgrade-maya-account/) first. Maya Savings is offered to upgraded accounts only.
2. Open the Maya app and tap **Savings** at the top of the screen.
3. Tap **Start Maya Savings** and follow the prompts.
4. Fund it by depositing from your wallet.

There is **no minimum balance** to open it.

## Putting money in and taking it out

| Action | What Maya says |
| --- | --- |
| Wallet to Savings | Instant and free |
| Savings to Wallet | Instant and free |
| Savings to a friend's Maya Savings | Instant and free |
| Other banks and e-wallets into Savings | Through InstaPay or PESONet, using your 12-digit Maya Savings account number. The sending bank sets its own fee |
| Savings out to other banks via InstaPay | ₱15 per transaction |
| Savings out via PESONet | Free until further notice |

PESONet has a cutoff. A transfer made before 4 PM is posted the same business day, and a later one waits for the next business day.

## Personal Goals

You can open up to **five Personal Goals**, each running up to **180 days**. Each one starts at **4% a year** and, according to Maya, the rate rises as the balance grows, up to **8%**. Interest is credited monthly on the first day of the next month. You need an active Maya Savings account to create one.

## Time Deposit Plus

Time Deposit Plus locks money for **3, 6 or 12 months** and pays a higher rate once you reach your target amount. The minimum target is ₱5,000. Cancelling early can trigger documentary stamp tax and a cancellation fee, and you lose the boosted interest, so only lock money you will not need.

## Fees and rules to check before you deposit

- Maya Bank can require a **minimum monthly average daily balance**. Missing it for two months in a row can bring a maintenance fee. Check the current fee table in the app.
- An account with **no deposit or withdrawal for two years is treated as dormant** and earns no interest. Dormancy fees may follow after five years.
- Closing an account within 30 days of opening can carry a small charge.
