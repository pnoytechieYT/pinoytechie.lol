MariBank advertises **3.25% per annum** on balances up to ₱1,000,000, credited daily, subject to **20% withholding tax**. The advertised rate is gross. What lands in your account is lower. Here is what that looks like at three common balance levels.

## The Tax Math

A 20% withholding tax applies to interest earned. The net rate is therefore **3.25% × 0.80 = 2.60%** per annum for balances under ₱1M. For the portion above ₱1M, the gross rate is 3.75%, and the net is **3.00%**.

MariBank's own example on its savings page shows that a **₱30,000** balance earns approximately **₱2,600** in net interest over one year, which corresponds to the 2.60% net rate. That confirms the tax treatment and the daily crediting method produce the expected result.

## ₱10,000

Gross annual interest: **₱325**
Net after 20% tax: **₱260**
Monthly average net: approximately **₱21.67**

At this balance, the peso difference between MariBank and a traditional bank paying 0.25% is small in absolute terms—about ₱240 per year. The value of MariBank at this level is more about the habit of saving separately from a spending account than about the interest itself.

## ₱50,000

Gross annual interest: **₱1,625**
Net after 20% tax: **₱1,300**
Monthly average net: approximately **₱108.33**

Here the difference from a traditional bank becomes noticeable. A 0.25% account would net about ₱100 per year; MariBank nets ₱1,300. That is roughly **₱1,200 in additional net interest** annually, or enough to cover a modest monthly bill.

## ₱100,000

Gross annual interest: **₱3,250**
Net after 20% tax: **₱2,600**
Monthly average net: approximately **₱216.67**

This is where the daily crediting becomes visible in the app. At ₱100,000, you are earning roughly **₱7.12 gross per day**, or about **₱5.70 net**. Over a month, the balance grows by a little over ₱170 net.

## What the Table Does Not Show

These figures assume the balance stays constant. In practice, if you add to the account regularly, the interest compounds on a growing base. If you withdraw frequently, the daily crediting works against you because each day's interest is based on the previous day's ending balance.

The calculations also assume the rate stays at 3.25%. MariBank has adjusted rates before—it lowered the rate in January 2026—so the actual earnings on a one-year horizon depend on future rate decisions. The figures above are a snapshot based on the current published rate, not a guarantee.
